The "Natural Sharing" Concept

Think of a service, tool, or local business you genuinely believe in because it solves a problem or improves a situation.

"If you saw someone struggling with the exact problem this thing solves, would you share it with them?"

The Goal: Understanding & Improving

We aren't here to make assumptions or guaranteed claims. Our mission is to understand a merchant's environment and see if there is a way to improve it.

Curiosity First

Asking questions to learn what their day-to-day looks like.

Situational Awareness

Identifying friction points that might be holding them back.

Scenario: The Complex Point of Sale

A small shop uses an incredibly advanced, high-tech payment system. The owner is proud of its capabilities but visibly stressed about the cost and underutilization.

The Discovery Question:

"How does a shop like yours typically handle payments?"

The Lesson

The merchant's tone reveals the problem long before the data does. You don't need to be an expert to recognize a mismatched solution.

☕

Scenario: The Manual Entry Bottleneck

A coffee shop with a massive line of cars. The employee has modern hardware but is manually typing in every 16-digit card number.

The Reality:

This is a friction point for the employee, the business, and the customer.

The Observation:

"I noticed you're keying those in manually—does that normally happen?"

Empathy for a difficult situation is often the most effective bridge to a meaningful conversation.

Understanding what to look for

Manual Processes

Hand-typing card numbers into systems. It slows down business and creates errors.

Cluttered Environments

Multiple tablets or screens on the counter that don't talk to each other.

Underused Solutions

High-cost systems being used for only basic functions. A sign of being over-sold.

Direct Pain Points

Taped-up signs about card minimums or extra fees to cover processing costs.

Operational Friction

Business owners stuck troubleshooting technology instead of running their shop.

Unique Setups

Recognizing a setup you haven't seen before is a chance to learn from the merchant.

Questions to ask

Open Curiosity

"How does a shop like yours typically handle payments?"

Specific Observation

"I noticed you're keying that in—does that normally happen?"

Impact Check

"If you could change one thing about your checkout, what would it be?"

Market Context

"Who do you use for processing? I'm curious what shops here prefer."

Making the Connection

A Sincere Approach:

"I work for Gravity. We focus on improving payment environments. If you're open to it, I'd love to see if we can help improve your situation."

If they say no, the relationship is still intact. You offered help, and they are okay.
