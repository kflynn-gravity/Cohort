The Insight Series

Learning to Observe

Lesson 01

Tone Reveals the Problem

Listen for the "Stress Shift."

Hardware pride often hides cost anxiety. Their tone will tell you they are oversold long before you see a single data sheet or bank statement.

Right-sizing begins with hearing the mismatch.

Lesson 02

Empathy for Friction

Expertise is not required to see a bottleneck.

When a process stresses staff and slows down business, it creates visible friction. If it looks like a pain in the neck, it probably is.

Empathy for their reality is your bridge to a conversation.

Lesson 03

Feedback as a Symptom

Complaints indicate a desire for change.

Hearing a third-party complaint (like a citizen talking about utility fees) is a clear indicator that something is wrong. You don't need to do the math to recognize a service gap.

Discovery starts by acknowledging the frustration.

Observation Framework

Environmental Indicators

Manual Entry

Hand-typing card numbers into terminals during checkout instead of swiping or tapping.

Disjointed Systems

Multiple disconnected screens or tablets on the counter that don't speak to one another.

Underutilized Tech

High-cost POS systems being used for only basic calculator or swipe functions.

Fee Signage

Handwritten notes about card minimums or extra fees taped to the register or counter.

Customer Feedback

Active dissatisfaction overheard from customers regarding the payment or checkout speed.

Unique Setups

Operational flows you haven't seen before—opportunity to learn how that niche works.

The Investigation

Starting the Conversation

Curiosity
