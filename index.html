We are here to help, and that starts with the permission to be curious.

The Concept of Natural Sharing

An Act of Service

Think of something you love that genuinely helps others. If you saw someone struggling, sharing it would be natural—not a pitch.

This is the root of the "Improvement Mindset." We share Gravity's solutions because they better a situation.

Core Skills to Help Merchants

Observation

Identify visual and verbal cues of friction or dissatisfaction in the environment.

Listening

Understand the merchant’s perspective and emotional tone regarding their setup.

It’s not about what you know, it’s about what you notice.

Lesson 1: Tone Reveals the Problem

The Insight: Listen for the "Stress Shift."

A merchant may take pride in their hardware but feel the weight of its cost or complexity. Their tone will tell you they are oversold long before you see data sheets or price points.

Right-sizing begins with hearing the mismatch.

Lesson 2: Empathy for Friction

The Insight: Expertise is not required to see a bottleneck.

When a process slows down business and stresses staff, it creates visible friction. If it looks like a struggle, it probably is.

Empathy for the worker's reality is your bridge to a conversation.

Lesson 3: Feedback as a Symptom

The Insight: Complaints indicate a desire for change.

Hearing a third-party complaint (like a citizen talking about utility fees) is a clear indicator that something is wrong. You don't need to do the math to recognize a service gap.

Discovery starts by acknowledging the frustration.

Understanding What to Look For

Manual Entry

Hand-typing card numbers into systems.

The Graveyard

Multiple disconnected screens on the counter.

Dusty Tech

Expensive POS systems used for basic functions.

Fee Signage

Taped-up signs about card minimums or fees.

Operational Friction

Owners troubleshooting tech instead of leading.

Unique Setups

Setups you haven't seen before—chance to learn.

Questions to Start Conversations

  • Curiosity: "How does a shop like yours typically handle payments?"
  • Empathy: "I noticed you're keying those in manually—does that normally happen?"
  • Impact: "If you could change one thing about your checkout, what would it be?"
  • Context: "Who do you use for processing? I'm curious what shops here prefer."
