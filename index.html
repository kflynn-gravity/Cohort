The Investigation

Three Stories

Story 01

The "Ferrari" Setup

A small board game shop with a massive, high-tech system. The owner is proud, but his tone changes when talking about the cost.

The Stress Shift: You didn't need a data sheet to hear he was oversold. Right-sizing begins with hearing the mismatch.

Story 02

The Manual Bottleneck

A busy coffee shop line. The barista is hand-typing 16-digit card numbers while customers wait. Does it look like a hassle?

The "Pain in the Neck" Rule: If it looks slow and annoying, it probably is. Expertise isn't required to see a bottleneck.

Story 03

The Fee Complaint

Overhearing a citizen complain about a utility fee at City Hall. You don't know the rate, but you hear the dissatisfaction.

Symptoms Matter: A complaint is an indicator of a gap. You don't need to do the math to recognize a desire for change.

The Best Case Scenario

What if they love it?

If a merchant says their setup is perfect, Celebrate with them.

We aren't looking to "snake" merchants away from good situations. If they are happy, learn from them.

"That's awesome! I love hearing that. What's the name of that system? I'd love to learn what they're doing right."

The Outcome

You’ve built a relationship, learned about a competitor's strength, and kept the mission of support first.

Field Guide

What to look for

Manual Entry

Hand-typing numbers into terminals instead of tapping.

Disjointed Systems

Multiple disconnected tablets that don't talk to each other.

Fee Signage

Handwritten notes about card minimums or extra fees.

Unique Setups

Opportunities to learn about niche operational flows.

  • 2

  • 3
    The Hand-off: Hit submit. An expert takes it from there.
